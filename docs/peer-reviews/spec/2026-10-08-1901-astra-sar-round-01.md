**SAR-1901-R1-01 — P1 — Define how mutable allocations coexist with immutable publication replay**

Specification references: lines 93–109, 143–149.

The specification requires preceding slices to belong to their chronological closing boundary, derives candidates against the fresh canonical source, includes allocations in replay equality, and preserves existing immutable journal payloads. These requirements need an explicit reconciliation rule when later evidence changes allocations already published.

Example: actor A starts at tick 0 and publishes an update at tick 20 containing 20 Active seconds. A delayed shared boundary recorded at tick 10 then arrives. The chronological result must allocate 10 seconds to that boundary and 10 to the update. Merely subtracting previously allocated coverage gives the boundary zero; adding its preceding slice without revising the update double-counts time. Revising the update then changes the allocation representation against which its original journal row will replay. Historical repair creates the same replay problem by changing duration cells/suffixes on already published rows.

Current evidence:

- `scripts/task-tracker/gh-timing-comment.mjs:592–623` compares actor markers and almost every cell, and throws `timing-evidence:conflicting-interval` when matching endpoints carry different row content.
- The same file, lines 643–650, inserts delayed actor rows chronologically.
- Its legacy path, lines 716–735, instead advances delayed row timestamps to the current tail. That behavior cannot remain authoritative for event-derived duration while preserving original event identity.
- `scripts/task-tracker/lib/actor-flush-journal.mjs` stores and validates the digest of the complete immutable payload, including the original row.

Proposed correction:

Specify two distinct comparisons: immutable source-event identity and the current derived projection. Define precisely which fields are immutable and which publisher-owned duration cells/suffixes may change. Replay of the same immutable source event must acknowledge its current valid projection without another interval credit; conflicting immutable event, actor, endpoint, word, description, or unrelated metadata must still fail.

Define the late-insertion policy explicitly. Recommended: preserve original timestamps and deterministically rederive affected allocations and duration cells in the same canonical-body mutation, with stable ordering for equal timestamps. If a protected source prefix prevents this mutation, refuse publication/reallocation with a diagnostic rather than moving timestamps or misallocating seconds.

Add acceptance cases for delayed actor and shared-boundary insertion, equal-second ordering, original journal replay after reallocation, original journal replay after repair, and conflicting immutable payloads. Include the sealed-source protection from R1-02.

**SAR-1901-R1-02 — P1 — Protect sealed outcome validation, not just sealed outcome bytes**

Specification references: lines 11, 99, 145–151.

“Do not rewrite sealed outcomes” does not prevent the proposed repair from invalidating them. Current incomplete-outcome validation binds both to exact live Timing Log prefix bytes and to the old derivation’s output. A display-only repair of that prefix can break validation even if timestamps, actors, words, and sealed outcome records remain untouched. Replacing the shared derivation used to validate old outcomes can also change their expected telemetry.

Current evidence:

- `scripts/task-tracker/lib/estimation/outcome-record.mjs:300–327` derives sealed telemetry through `deriveActorEngagement` and stores the exact snapshot digest.
- Lines 351–380 recompute that telemetry and require canonical equality.
- Lines 408–418 require the live Timing Log body to begin with the exact sealed snapshot prefix.
- `scripts/task-tracker/lib/estimation/runtime-adapter.mjs` calls `validateOutcomeTimingSource` when reusing/checking outcomes, including at lines 1488 and 1554.

Proposed correction:

Add an explicit compatibility boundary: existing sealed schema versions retain their original validation and derivation semantics. New event-derived projections must not silently redefine the calculations used to validate old sealed records.

Before any historical apply or publication-driven reallocation, detect whether changed bytes belong to a source prefix referenced by sealed outcome evidence. The bounded solution for this defect is preview/export only for that protected change, reporting the blocking record and prefix. Do not rewrite the record or weaken its validator. Supporting authoritative repair lineage across sealed prefixes would require an explicitly designed and authorized extension; it should not be inferred from the existing repair permission.

Add tests proving that an existing sealed outcome still validates after reader rollout, that repairs touching its protected prefix refuse mutation, and that permitted append-only successors retain existing lineage validation.

**SAR-1901-R1-03 — P2 — Specify the formulas for Plan, Review, and Engaged projections**

Specification references: lines 56, 97–99, 162.

“Review/Plan projections use these allocations and existing stage definitions” does not select a single existing numeric meaning. The current legacy and actor paths disagree, and the specification does not say whether these projections sum Active, Idle, or both. It also does not settle completeness for an open stage with already closed slices.

Current evidence:

- `scripts/task-tracker/timing-rollup.mjs:92–108` derives legacy Review from short pause-to-next-row gaps, regardless of Review-stage membership.
- Lines 163–179 derive legacy Plan from closed stage wall time, including interruptions, rounded per visit.
- Lines 307–325 derive actor Plan from engagement duration and leave Review unavailable.
- Lines 368–380 add the legacy Review heuristic to Active to form Engaged.

The specification correctly rejects adding old heuristic totals, but that prohibition alone leaves multiple plausible replacements. For a Plan visit containing 120 Active seconds and 180 Idle seconds, the possible Plan outputs include two minutes, five minutes, or Unknown.

Proposed correction:

Add a compact field contract specifying the slice dimension, stage filter, completeness rule, open-visit treatment, and seconds-to-minutes conversion for every affected public scalar.

A consistent proposed contract is:

- Active and Engaged both sum known Active allocations; Review is a subset and is never added again.
- Plan and Review sum Active allocations assigned to their respective existing stage visits.
- Idle remains separately available by stage and issue.
- Closed slices in an ongoing visit contribute; an unobserved future tail contributes nothing rather than making those closed slices unknown.
- Unknown propagates only where an unavailable portion can affect the requested projection, with its known subtotal exposed separately.
- Aggregate seconds before the selected existing board minute conversion; do not mix per-visit rounding and fractional-minute behavior.

If different field semantics are intended, state those formulas instead. Add legacy/actor parity cases with interrupted Plan/Review visits, repeated visits, open visits, and uncertainty confined to another stage.

The core design is otherwise well aligned with the defect: transcript-independent elapsed timing, whole-second endpoint normalization, actor isolation, the narrow legacy bridge, shared boundary partitioning, composed metadata, and guarded repair are all addressed. No optional preference findings are requested.
