Investigate a recovery path for a blocker discovered after an epic child has entered Develop under the strict one-active-child WIP rule. This is a backlog investigation, not approval to relax the guard.

Concrete incident: in epic #1558, child #1676 is in Develop and is blocked by newly discovered defect #1765. Defect #1765 is a separate child in Ready for Planning. Its Plan entry refuses because #1676 still consumes the epic WIP slot. #1676 cannot honestly complete its feasibility recheck until #1765 corrects the #1675 lifecycle capture. Moving the defect back into #1676 would obscure its separate ownership; detaching #1765 would route its delivery to trunk, while the fix is needed on the #1558 epic branch.

The rule is deliberate. #1216 established sequential local child execution pending proven isolated cloud CI, superseding the older parked-blocker exception described in #247. The current guard counts blocked active siblings, and the regression test explicitly refuses a project-config bypass. The #1558 approved plan also says not to weaken guards and to admit one child at a time. These are safety and provenance fences, not incidental friction.

When revisited, compare recovery approaches against those authorities: an honest way to park and later resume an active child, a separately governed blocker handoff with the stated CI prerequisite, or a dependency/decomposition change that preserves truthful acceptance evidence. Identify the effect on issue states, exact-SHA receipts, ownership, merge-back, and audit history. Do not implement a one-off bypass or mark #1676 Done with an unmet GO criterion.
